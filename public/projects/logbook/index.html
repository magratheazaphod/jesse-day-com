Logbook

One page for "what should I be doing" and "what did I actually do".

Once most of my building ran through Claude Code, I had a new problem: a day could hold a dozen agent sessions across four projects, and by evening I couldn't say what had actually happened. My to-do list lived in one place and the record of the work lived in transcripts nobody reads.

Logbook puts both on one screen. On the left, a backlog, an ideas list and a content list for article and video topics. On the right, a daily log that writes itself: it reads that day's Claude Code transcripts into a ledger of sessions, grouped by project, with times, git branch and prompt counts, and a one-sentence summary of each session written in the background by my own claude CLI.

The design choice I care most about is what happens at midnight. Unfinished focus items don't linger on a past day; they roll back to the top of the backlog. So stepping back to any earlier date shows only what actually got done, plus a one-line summary of the whole day.

It is deliberately small: Python's standard library and one HTML file, no dependencies, no build step, no account. It runs on your own machine and makes no network calls of its own. The board is plain JSON, so an agent or a cron job can add to it directly, with a revision counter so a stale browser tab can't clobber newer saves.

Logbook is open source under the MIT license: github.com/magratheazaphod/logbook.
